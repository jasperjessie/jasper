## The problem this solves

Most schools track academic performance obsessively — grades, test scores, attendance — but have no structured way to measure the social-emotional side of a child's growth. Feedback on that side tends to be anecdotal ("she's doing great," "he's a bit withdrawn"), which means it can't be compared across students, tracked over time, or acted on with any precision. Programs end up unable to answer a basic question: *which specific children need targeted support, in which specific skill, right now?*

## Framework I use: DESSA-based SEL assessment

I build structured social-emotional learning (SEL) assessment systems adapted from the **DESSA** (Devereux Student Strengths Assessment) framework — a strengths-based model that scores a child across five competency domains using behaviorally specific, observable indicators rather than vague impressions.

Each domain is scored from a set of concrete, observable items (rated *Never* to *Very Frequently*), which keeps the assessment behavioral rather than subjective — facilitators are rating what they saw, not forming a general impression.

## What this makes possible for a school or program

- **Move from anecdotal notes to comparable scores** — every child assessed on the same five domains, the same way, every time.
- **Flag who needs targeted instruction** — instead of guessing, the "Needs Instruction" band identifies specific domains for specific children.
- **Generate an individual skill map** — each student's five domain scores plotted as a profile, making strengths and gaps visible at a glance to teachers, facilitators, and parents.
- **Track growth over a term or year** — repeat the assessment and compare, turning SEL from a one-time snapshot into a longitudinal record.
- **Give facilitators a shared vocabulary** — structured domains and indicators replace inconsistent, facilitator-dependent language in reports home.

## How an engagement typically runs

1. Adapt the DESSA item set and scoring bands to the program's age group and context.
2. Train facilitators/teachers on behaviorally-specific rating (what counts as "frequently" vs. "occasionally").
3. Run the assessment at a defined cadence (e.g., start and end of term).
4. Turn raw scores into individual reports and program-level dashboards.
5. Review results with staff to translate scores into concrete next steps per child.

This same structured-assessment approach — defining clear competency domains, observable indicators, and interpretable score bands — is the same thinking behind [Competency Framework Design](/services/competency-framework-design), just applied specifically to the social-emotional domain.
