## The Problem

For over a century, education has largely measured learning through:

- attendance
- credit hours
- exam scores
- Course completion
- Examination scores

## The Problem with Time-Based Education

Imagine two students enrolled in the same course.

- One grasps concepts quickly and can apply them confidently.
- The other requires additional practice and feedback before demonstrating proficiency.

Yet, both spend the same number of hours in class, complete the same assignments, and receive a grade at the end of the semester.

!image.png

Traditional education has historically measured learning through proxies:

- Attendance
- Credit hours
- Course completion
- Examination scores

But these metrics do not necessarily answer the most important question:

<aside>

> ***Can the learner actually perform the required task or demonstrate the required capability?***
> 
</aside>

This question lies at the heart of **Competency-Based Education (CBE)**.

## What is Competency-Based Education?

According to educational researcher Benjamin Bloom, learning outcomes improve significantly when learners are provided adequate time, feedback, and support to achieve mastery.

Competency-Based Education builds upon this principle.

Rather than measuring learning by time spent in a classroom, CBE measures learning by the demonstration of clearly defined competencies.

A competency is typically understood as:

```jsx
Knowledge
+
Skills
+
Attitudes / Behaviours
= Competency
```

In a CBE system, learners progress when they can demonstrate mastery—not simply because a semester has ended.

## The Origins of Competency-Based Education

The roots of CBE can be traced to several influential thinkers.

### Benjamin Bloom and Mastery Learning

In his landmark work on Mastery Learning, Bloom argued that nearly all students can achieve high levels of learning when provided sufficient time and targeted feedback.

Recommended Reading:

Human Characteristics and School Learning

### Lev Vygotsky and the Zone of Proximal Development

Lev Vygotsky proposed that learning occurs most effectively when learners receive support just beyond their current level of ability.

!image.png

!image.png

This idea strongly aligns with personalized progression pathways found in modern CBE systems.

Recommended Reading:

Mind in Society

---

### John Dewey and Learning by Doing

John Dewey advocated experiential learning and argued that education must connect directly with real-world experiences.

Many competency-based programs incorporate projects, portfolios, simulations, and authentic assessments inspired by Dewey's philosophy.
